# English Legal Academic Writing Guide

> A comprehensive guide for writing English legal academic papers for SSCI journal submission. Covers IRAC/CREAC methods, Common Law vs Civil Law writing traditions, and English-specific de-AI patterns.

---

## 一、English Legal Academic Writing Fundamentals

### 1.1 IRAC Method (Issue, Rule, Application, Conclusion)

The gold standard for legal analysis in English academic writing. Every analytical paragraph should ideally follow this structure:

```
ISSUE:    What is the specific legal question?
          → One sentence, framed as a question or a statement of the issue.
RULE:     What legal rule governs this issue?
          → Cite the relevant statute, case, or scholarly authority.
APPLICATION: How does the rule apply to the facts/case at hand?
          → This is the core of your analysis. Be specific and thorough.
CONCLUSION: What is the outcome?
          → One or two sentences stating the conclusion.
```

**Example paragraph:**

> The issue is whether Article 58 of the PRC Contract Law permits a party to terminate a contract when the other party's breach renders the contract purpose unachievable. Article 58 provides that a party may terminate a contract if the other party's breach "renders it impossible to achieve the purpose of the contract." Applying this provision to Platform Co.'s systematic failure to deliver goods for three consecutive months, the breach clearly falls within the scope of Article 58 because the buyer's purpose — obtaining goods within a reasonable time — has been entirely defeated. Therefore, the buyer is entitled to terminate the contract under Article 58.

### 1.2 CREAC Variation (Conclusion, Rule, Explanation, Application, Conclusion)

Preferred by many top U.S. law reviews. The key difference: start with your conclusion upfront.

```
CONCLUSION:  State your bottom line first.
RULE:        State the governing rule.
EXPLANATION: Explain why the rule leads to this conclusion.
APPLICATION: Apply to specific facts/cases.
CONCLUSION:  Restate (briefly) or transition to next point.
```

CREAC is more persuasive because the reader knows your position from the start. IRAC is more neutral and exploratory.

### 1.3 How English Legal Writing Differs from Chinese Legal Writing

| Aspect | Chinese Legal Writing | English Legal Writing |
|--------|---------------------|---------------------|
| **Thesis placement** | Often builds up to conclusion | States thesis upfront (CREAC) |
| **Topic sentences** | May be implicit | Must be explicit at paragraph start |
| **Paragraph length** | 200-500 characters (longer OK) | 100-200 words (shorter preferred) |
| **Passive voice** | Common and acceptable | Prefer active voice |
| **Transitions** | Often use "首先/其次/再次" | Use logical connectors (However, Moreover, Thus) |
| **Case citations** | Case name + case number | Full Bluebook citation with reporter |
| **Hedging** | Sometimes underused | Use strategic hedging ("arguably", "it appears") |
| **Directness** | Indirect, building arguments | Direct, leading with conclusions |

### 1.4 Active vs Passive Voice in English Legal Writing

**Prefer active:**
- ✅ "The Supreme Court held that..." (not "It was held by the Supreme Court that...")
- ✅ "The plaintiff argued..." (not "It was argued by the plaintiff that...")
- ✅ "This Article proposes..." (not "It is proposed in this Article that...")

**Acceptable passive (limited):**
- Legal rule statements: "The contract was terminated on January 15."
- When the actor is unknown or irrelevant: "The case was appealed."
- When emphasizing the action over the actor

## 二、SSCI Legal Journal Writing Guide

### 2.1 Standard Paper Structure

```
Abstract (250-300 words, structured)
  → Purpose, Method, Findings, Significance
  → Keywords (5-8 terms)

1. Introduction (10-15% of paper)
  → Hook + context
  → Research question
  → Literature gap
  → Contribution statement (1-2 sentences)
  → Roadmap

2. Literature Review (15-25%)
  → Thematic organization (NOT chronological unless historical study)
  → Identify gap explicitly

3. Analysis / Main Body (40-50%)
  → Multiple subsections
  → Each subsection follows IRAC/CREAC
  → Compare jurisdictions where appropriate

4. Discussion (10-15%)
  → Policy implications
  → Broader significance
  → Limitations

5. Conclusion (5-10%)
  → Restate contribution (briefly)
  → Future research directions
```

### 2.2 Writing a Good Abstract

**Structure (follow this order):**
1. **Purpose** (1-2 sentences): "This Article examines..."
2. **Method** (1-2 sentences): "Drawing on analysis of X cases / comparative study of Y jurisdictions..."
3. **Findings** (2-3 sentences): "It argues that... The analysis reveals..."
4. **Significance** (1-2 sentences): "These findings contribute to..."

**Common abstract mistakes:**
- Too vague: "This paper discusses contract law issues." → "This Article examines the applicability of Article 58 of the PRC Contract Law to platform economy disputes."
- Too long: Keep under 300 words for most journals
- Missing keywords: Always include 5-8 keywords

### 2.3 Contribution Statement

A contribution statement should be concrete and specific:

> **Weak:** "This paper contributes to the literature on contract law."
> **Strong:** "This Article makes two contributions. First, it identifies a systematic inconsistency in Chinese courts' application of Article 58, drawing on analysis of 247 appellate decisions. Second, it proposes a three-factor test for determining when breach renders a contract's purpose unachievable."

### 2.4 Policy Implications Section

Many SSCI journals expect a section on policy implications. Structure:

```
- Implication 1: For legislators (specific statutory amendment suggestions)
- Implication 2: For courts (judicial guidance)
- Implication 3: For practitioners (practical guidance)
- Cross-jurisdictional implications (if comparative study)
```

## 三、Common Law vs Civil Law Writing Traditions

### 3.1 Key Differences

| Dimension | Common Law Tradition | Civil Law Tradition |
|-----------|---------------------|-------------------|
| **Primary authority** | Case law (precedent) | Statutory law (codes) |
| **Citation emphasis** | Case names and reporter citations | Code articles and paragraph numbers |
| **Argument style** | Case-by-case reasoning, analogical | Systematic, deductive from code provisions |
| **Use of cases** | Extensive case analysis central | Cases as supplementary support |
| **Structure** | Often follows fact pattern | Often follows logical categories |
| **Tone** | More conversational in some U.S. journals | More formal and systematic |

### 3.2 Writing for an International Audience

When writing about Chinese law for an international SSCI audience:

**Do:**
- Explain Chinese legal concepts in terms an international reader can understand
- Provide the Chinese term in Pinyin + characters on first use: "the concept of 物权 (wuquan, property rights)"
- Include English translations of cited Chinese statutes (or note if no official translation exists)
- Briefly explain unique Chinese legal institutions (e.g., guiding cases, judicial interpretations)

**Don't:**
- Assume readers know Chinese legal terminology
- Use Chinese terms without explanation
- Over-explain basic legal concepts that are universal
- Mix Chinese and English legal terminology inconsistently

### 3.3 Translating Chinese Legal Terms

| Chinese Term | Standard English Translation | Common Mistake |
|-------------|---------------------------|----------------|
| 物权 | Property rights / Real rights | "Material rights" |
| 债权 | Obligatory rights / Claims | "Debt rights" |
| 法人 | Legal person | "Legal entity" (acceptable but less precise) |
| 诉讼时效 | Statute of limitations | "Prescription period" |
| 不可抗力 | Force majeure | "Irresistible force" |
| 连带责任 | Joint and several liability | "Connected responsibility" |
| 指导性案例 | Guiding cases | "Guidance cases" / "Guiding precedents" |
| 司法解释 | Judicial interpretation | "Judicial explanation" |
| 缔约过失 | Culpa in contrahendo | "Fault in contracting" |
| 善意取得 | Bona fide acquisition | "Good faith purchase" |

## 四、English De-AI Patterns for Legal Writing

### 4.1 AI Patterns in English Legal Writing (18 patterns)

#### Content Patterns

| # | Pattern | Example | Fix |
|---|---------|---------|-----|
| 1 | Overblown significance | "This case represents a watershed moment in..." | Use specific, measured language |
| 2 | Vague contribution claims | "This paper makes a significant contribution to..." | Specify the actual contribution |
| 3 | Generic policy relevance | "This has important implications for legal reform" | Specify what reform and how |
| 4 | False attribution | "Scholars generally agree that..." | "Zhang (2020) argues that..." |

#### Language Patterns

| # | Pattern | AI Flag Words | Fix |
|---|---------|--------------|-----|
| 5 | Formulaic transitions | Furthermore, Moreover, In addition, Additionally | Vary or eliminate; use content-based transitions |
| 6 | Hedging overload | arguably, it could be contended, one might suggest | Use strategic, not excessive hedging |
| 7 | Robotic opening | "In recent years, [topic] has attracted significant attention" | Open with the specific problem or question |
| 8 | Conclusion formula | "In conclusion, it is clear that..." | State the conclusion directly without preamble |
| 9 | Overuse of "therefore" | 3+ times per article | Vary: thus, consequently, as a result, or restructure |
| 10 | "It is worth noting" | "It is worth noting that..." | Delete; state what's worth noting directly |

#### Structural Patterns

| # | Pattern | Description | Fix |
|---|---------|-------------|-----|
| 11 | Symmetric balancing | "On the one hand... on the other hand..." repeated | Take a position |
| 12 | Numbered lists in prose | "First... Second... Third... Fourth..." mechanical | Use thematic transitions |
| 13 | Uniform paragraph length | Every paragraph 150-180 words | Vary: core paragraphs longer, transitions shorter |

#### Filler Patterns

| # | Pattern | Example | Fix |
|---|---------|---------|-----|
| 14 | Filler openings | "It is important to recognize that..." | Delete |
| 15 | Empty promises | "Further research is needed to explore..." | Specify what research on what question |
| 16 | Grandiose endings | "...will contribute to the development of legal theory" | Be specific about contribution |

### 4.2 English AI Vocabulary Checklist

The following words/phrases appear disproportionately in AI-generated legal texts. Density of 3+ per paragraph is suspicious:

> Furthermore, Moreover, In addition, Additionally, It is worth noting, It is important to emphasize, Consequently, Nevertheless, Notwithstanding, It should be noted, One might argue, It could be contended, In this context, Against this backdrop, plays a crucial role, has significant implications, comprehensive analysis, thorough examination, paradigm shift, groundbreaking, unprecedented, raises important questions, deserves careful consideration

### 4.3 English De-AI Workflow

```
Step 1: Identify
  → Run the vocabulary density check (≤1 flagged word per paragraph)
  → Check for formulaic transitions and hedging overload
  → Flag vague attribution and overblown significance

Step 2: Rewrite
  → Replace formulaic transitions with content-based connections
  → Remove filler phrases entirely
  → Add specific details where language is vague
  → Maintain formal academic tone (do NOT make it casual)

Step 3: Audit
  → "Would a law professor suspect this was AI-written?"
  → Check paragraph length variation
  → Verify thesis is upfront (CREAC structure)

Step 4: Final Polish
  → Ensure Bluebook citations are correct
  → Verify all legal terms are precisely used
  → Check that Chinese legal concepts are properly explained
```

## 五、Quick Reference: Weak → Strong Phrases

| Weak Phrase | Stronger Alternative |
|------------|---------------------|
| It is important to note that... | [Delete] State the point directly |
| In recent years, X has become important | [Start with the specific problem] |
| Scholars have argued that... | Scholar A (2020) argues that... |
| This paper makes a significant contribution | This Article identifies [specific finding] and proposes [specific test] |
| Furthermore, ... | [Restate the logical connection explicitly] |
| One could argue that... | [Just make the argument directly] |
| This case is groundbreaking | This case established [specific rule] that has been followed in [N jurisdictions] |
| A comprehensive analysis reveals that... | Analysis of [specific data/cases] shows that... |
| There are several reasons for this | Three factors explain this: [list them] |
| In conclusion, this paper has shown... | This Article has demonstrated [specific finding], which suggests [implication] |
